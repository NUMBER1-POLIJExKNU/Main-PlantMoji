## The one that needs a decision, not a resolution

Both sides independently found and fixed the same bug — four moods were
pointed at the pack's `plain` file, which is the designer's **faceless**
decorative body (leaf veins where the face goes), so a plant that went cold
or off-pH lost its face entirely. The fixes disagree:

| Mood | main | this branch |
|---|---|---|
| TooCold | `sleepy` | `happy` |
| HumidAir | `unwell` | `happy` |
| SoilAcidic | `unwell` | `happy` |
| SoilAlkaline | `unwell` | `happy` |
| Sleepy | `sleepy` | `happy` |

Taking the branch's side makes Jamkachu smile through every problem state,
and smile at night instead of sleeping. That contradicts the loop the whole
product rests on — the face is supposed to report what the sensors actually
say, so a student can look at the character and know something needs doing.
A smiling plant on a bad reading also quietly breaks the quest story: the
mission says fix the soil, the character says everything is fine.

main's side keeps every mood on a face that matches its state. It needed one
new expression, since the pack only draws four faces and none of them read
as "mildly unwell": `unwell` is the designer's own thirsty frown wearing
their sleepy lids, generated by `scripts/build-unwell-face.mjs` (21 frames,
all phases and tiers). It is explicitly ours, not the designer's — swap the
files the moment they draw a real one. The moods that share a face are told
apart by the designer's mood badges beside the head (`assets/moods/`), so
all eight stay distinguishable.

**Recommendation:** take main's mapping in `src/lib/jamkachu-sprite.ts` and
`public/farm/jamkachu-sprite.js`, and this branch's side of everything else
in those files. `tests/jamkachu-sprite-assets.test.ts` pins the table
literally and fails if any mood points at `plain` again, so the merge cannot
silently land the faceless body a second time.

## Two things worth a second look before merging

**`jamkachu-home-portrait.png` is 1.45 MB.** It is `display:none` until the
desktop breakpoint, but browsers generally fetch hidden images anyway, so
every phone on a school network pays for it on first paint. The same call
already went the other way once this week: the combined title art was
rejected at 463 KB for a 44px slot and shipped as small derivatives instead
(commit `9ae5770`). Worth either a downscaled derivative or a `loading="lazy"`
+ breakpoint-gated `<picture>` source.

**`supabase/milestone21-level-evolution.sql` is a new migration.** Merging
the code without running it in Supabase leaves the new level-evolution
behaviour dead in production. It should join the go-live checklist in
`docs/RUNBOOK-filming-and-golive.md` alongside milestones 16–20.
